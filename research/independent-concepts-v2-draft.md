# Independent concept slate — 2026-10-23
Status: Generated from task constraints before new sponsor-document or competitor searches in this turn. These are our design proposals, not claims of worldwide novelty. Prior research in conversation is part of our context, so this is not a blind invention experiment.

User constraint: meaningfully use PayPal, AI, and all ten named sponsors. Focus original concepts first; do not extend PayGuard or the earlier recommended shortlist as the starting point.

1. Assembly: compile an outcome ("build a streaming workstation") into a typed dependency graph; discover products, prove compatibility and project completeness, then stage individually approved purchases. Hard problem: uncertain natural-language product claims becoming evidence-backed constraints, plus invalidating a plan when one component changes.
2. Pact: assemble temporary purchasing coalitions among small businesses with complementary needs; solve bundles, quantities, timing and member approval constraints. Hard problem: a member drops out after others commit; deterministic re-clearing and consent to changed prices, with explicit partial-settlement behavior.
3. StockSwap: turn idle merchant inventory into an exchange of real bilateral purchases; discover multi-party opportunities with product equivalence and time windows. Hard problem: graph cycles help discovery, but ordinary PayPal purchases are not atomic cross-party settlement.
4. ChangeOrder: compile changes to customer-approved project scope into a human-readable and machine-checkable diff of work, products, dates and exact incremental payment. Hard problem: detect which acceptance and purchases a changed requirement invalidates.
5. WaitWorth: a purchase agent that explicitly prices uncertainty and chooses whether to inspect, request evidence, buy a sample, wait or buy now. Hard problem: decide which next observation could actually change the decision under a budget and deadline, without inventing probabilities.
6. Promise Market: compare offers by executable delivery and compatibility commitments, not sticker price; construct a personalized frontier of cost, deadline and failure remedies. Hard problem: conditional promises are measurable but cannot create legal guarantees or automated compensation from unconsented sellers.
7. Checkout Wind Tunnel: generate a semantic model of a merchant checkout and synthesize adversarial purchase journeys, browser interactions and payment event faults; replay minimal failures into reproducible integration tests. Hard problem: causal minimization across browser, API and webhook state.
8. Margin Compiler: translate a custom-order conversation into a costed build plan with evidence-backed parts, labor, fees and uncertainty bounds; generate a quote whose exclusions and change triggers are executable. Hard problem: a feasible low-cost BOM may fail delivery or margin constraints.
9. Access Assembly: compile a person's practical accessibility needs into a verified combination of compatible devices, services and setup sessions; make unknown compatibility visible before checkout. Hard problem: personalized functional requirements exceed product keyword categories, with safety-critical claims requiring human confirmation.
10. Repair Commons: pool local repair jobs by shared equipment, technician route and compatible spares; buy shared supplies only after a viable service batch is confirmed. Hard problem: batching discounts versus repair urgency, with individual consent and actual purchases rather than pooled custody.
11. Offer Revision Ledger: preserve the exact commercial facts and source evidence used by an agent, detect material changes between recommendation, approval and checkout, and generate the smallest necessary re-decision. Hard problem: semantic changes (warranty, variant, delivery) exceed numerical amount changes; distinct from generic budget authorization.
12. Capacity Exchange: match a small seller's unfilled customer jobs against nearby idle machines or labor and required supplies; generate a schedule-backed subcontract offer and invoice. Hard problem: compositional feasibility and revealed capacity reliability; production fulfillment is not guaranteed by sandbox payment.

Selection questions (qualitative, no invented scores): Is the problem legible in 15 seconds? Is there a deep technical mechanism demonstrable in 60 seconds? Is PayPal essential to the demonstrated outcome? Can all sponsor roles operate naturally? Does the end-to-end slice fit two people and the remaining deadline?
