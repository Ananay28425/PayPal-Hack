# Assembly Proof submission decision

**Keep the Assembly-led project.** Sell it as a preflight check for multi-item agent carts. The buyer specifies the outcome, Assembly catches an incompatibility across items, and PayGuard binds the checked repair to the order the buyer approves. Counterexample supplies independently specified failures and recovery tests. The differentiator is the link between a real configuration decision and the eventual payment.

## Competition and fit

Devpost showed roughly 29.7k registrations during the November 6 review. Registration does not reveal how many eligible projects will be judged or how strong those projects will be. The site awards $12,000 for first place and evaluates five equally weighted criteria. The current local demo is a credible direction with incomplete submission evidence. | Direction | What the judge sees | Main evidence burden |
|---|---|---|
| Generic shopping agent | Chat, products, and checkout | PayPal already describes those capabilities in Agent Ready and Store Sync. The demo would need a new commercial outcome. |
| PayGuard as the lead | Spending consent and execution safeguards | PayPal's AP2 work covers verifiable intent and mandates. A distinctive failure and improvement would have to be proven independently. |
| Counterexample as the lead | Fault injection and regression results | Judges would still need to see why a buyer or merchant wants to adopt the product. |
| **Assembly-led with bounded controls** | One plausible cart fails, one repair passes, and the exact repaired offer reaches payment | Ground the variants and commercial terms, demonstrate live sandbox capture, and show that the checker generalizes to unseen fixtures. |

PayPal describes Store Sync for catalogue discovery and order placement and Agent Ready for accepting AI-assisted payments. Assembly Proof could fit upstream of those services by checking whether items work together before checkout. The current app uses Orders v2, and any future Agent Ready integration depends on PayPal's supported merchant, region, and currency scope. The project does not estimate a share of PayPal's reported transaction losses. [PayPal commerce services](https://developer.paypal.com/agentic-commerce-services/about/) · [Agent Ready](https://developer.paypal.com/agent-ready/overview/) · [PayPal's AP2 announcement](https://developer.paypal.com/community/blog/PayPal-Agent-Payments-Protocol/)

Retailers already sell preassembled four-person bundles, and research studies catalogue-grounded, verifiable multi-item shopping. The originality case is the preflight at the moment an agent builds or changes a custom cart. Show a purchase-breaking interaction, a repair that preserves the buyer's constraints, and a checkout that rejects stale terms. Compare against both an off-the-shelf bundle and the same planner without the checker. ## What each criterion needs

| Criterion | Present evidence | Evidence that would strengthen the entry |
|---|---|---|
| Implementation | Deterministic repair, independent checks, itemized PayPal order adapter, approval binding, local replay and timeout tests | A continuous run with live AI, PayPal Sandbox buyer approval and verified capture, then one independently reproduced fault |
| Design | Warm light UI starts with a concrete buyer problem, then shows the failure, price delta, alternatives, and frozen review | A first-time buyer can explain why the cheap kit fails and which exact offer they approved |
| Potential impact | One specific costly mistake prevented on an invented catalogue | One merchant-backed variant slice, real support or return reasons if available, and a blinded comparison against the same planner alone |
| Innovation | A changed proof invalidates checkout preparation, even at the same price | Held-out examples where evidence changes the decision, including a real variant ambiguity and a case with no affordable repair |
| Presentation | One continuous buyer path is available locally | A public, under-three-minute video showing the product and capture, with a working judge URL or reproducible setup |

## Evidence to collect before submission

1. Freeze one USD catalogue for one authorized merchant and one type of purchase. Tie each relevant fact to a specific variant, source, and revision. Distinguish merchant price and stock from manufacturer specifications. For example, Focusrite publishes the 2i2's two XLR connectors and its single headphone output. This establishes a real-world failure pattern, while the demo's $610 and $810 prices are invented. [Focusrite specifications](https://userguides.focusrite.com/hc/en-gb/articles/19640392541202-Scarlett-2i2-4th-Gen-Specifications)
2. Evaluate the same unreviewed planner outputs with and without the checker. Set the expected outcomes independently before the run. Include a valid kit, a cheaper invalid one, unknown attributes, wrong variants, a tight budget, and a compatible configuration with an unnecessary expensive item. Report raw counts and fixture details. The bad-kit escape rate is invalid kits approved divided by invalid kits tested. Also report valid kits incorrectly blocked and successfully repaired eligible failures, each with its own denominator. Record time to a correct choice in a small first-time buyer test. Sales lift and fewer returns are merchant pilot outcomes to measure later.
3. Record one clean Sandbox flow with real buyer approval and the resulting capture ID. Separately demonstrate one stale-proof rejection and one ambiguous capture that resolves against the same original order. Keep the simulated case clearly labeled if the external failure is injected.
4. Verify every sponsor with the pass condition in [the implementation ledger](../SPONSORS.md). Keep credentials server-side and a trace, request, or test output ready for judges. Keep the single successful customer journey in the video.

Suggested video beats within 2:50. At 0:00 show the buyer goal and budget. At 0:20 show the agent's proposed kit and the capacity failure. At 0:50 compare candidates and approve the computed repair. At 1:20 show the itemized buyer review and PayPal Sandbox. At 2:05 show the verified result and one stale-approval or replay check. Use the remaining time to identify the merchant pilot and point to the runnable build. Submit by **November 12, 2026 at noon Pacific time**, which is **November 13 at 1:30 a.m. IST**. The organizer calls for a public repository with an open-source license, a functional demo or complete run instructions, disclosure of AI tools, and a public YouTube video under three minutes. Keep the build accessible through winner announcement. ## Path to an authorized merchant pilot

- **Reliability and concurrency.** Start with one regional PostgreSQL writer, short transactions, and unique operation keys. Commit the operation and its durable request ID before calling PayPal. Use bounded retries within the provider's documented retention window and reconcile the original operation on uncertainty. Make webhook ingestion authenticated, replay-safe, and auditable. Test same-intent races, duplicates, process restarts, failover, and backup restoration against a real Postgres deployment. [PayPal idempotency](https://developer.paypal.com/api/rest/reference/idempotency/) · [PayPal webhook verification](https://developer.paypal.com/api/rest/webhooks/rest/)
- **Surges and outages.** Scale stateless reads and inference separately. Apply per-tenant quotas, bound queued work, and keep evidence refresh and fulfillment off the capture request. During an authoritative database partition, pause writes and allow clearly marked read-only status. Budget end-to-end latency with real provider limits and measure p95 checkout latency, queue age, and reconciliation lag at a declared load. Add merchant-affine regional cells after demand or residency requires them.
- **Trust boundaries.** Add merchant onboarding and buyer authentication, authorize each purchase against its tenant and owner, validate order identity and terms before capture, rotate and scope credentials, and redact telemetry. Limit evidence fetching to authorized sources and treat extracted text as data. Unverified specs and stale offers require buyer-visible review. [OWASP API Security](https://owasp.org/www-project-api-security/) · [OWASP GenAI Security](https://genai.owasp.org/)
- **Market rollout.** PayPal handles funds. Assembly Proof can first integrate with one merchant's inventory, delivery promises, returns, and support. Before opening a new market, validate localized currency precision, taxes, shipping, buyer approval and authentication requirements, and provider support. Compare verified checkout completion and incorrect-kit outcomes against a baseline. Estimate merchant economics from incremental contribution on completed orders and avoided return and support costs, less inference, integration, and operating costs.

These are pilot gates. The current source supports a local simulated flow and a configured Sandbox adapter. A production deployment requires access, security review, and measured operating evidence.
